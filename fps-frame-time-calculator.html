FPS & Frame Time Calculator

See how FPS translates into milliseconds per frame—a useful way to understand rendering responsiveness.

Convert FPS and frame time

60 FPS ≈ 16.67 ms per frame.

Why frame time matters

FPS is a rate; frame time is the time budget for one frame. The conversion is frame time in milliseconds = 1000 ÷ FPS. A stable 120 FPS is about 8.33 ms per frame, while 240 FPS is about 4.17 ms. Lower frame time means frames are produced more frequently, but perceived smoothness also depends on how consistent those frame times are.

Common FPS and frame-time examples

30 FPS is about 33.33 ms per frame, 60 FPS is 16.67 ms, 120 FPS is 8.33 ms, 144 FPS is about 6.94 ms, and 240 FPS is about 4.17 ms. These values are useful as a quick rendering budget. For example, a game targeting 120 FPS has roughly 8.33 ms to prepare each frame before the next frame interval arrives.

Average FPS is not the whole story

Two systems can report the same average FPS and still feel different. If one alternates between very fast and very slow frames, the average may look healthy while motion feels uneven. This calculator converts a single FPS or frame-time value; it does not calculate frame-time variance, 1% lows, 0.1% lows or stutter frequency from a benchmark log.

FPS and monitor refresh rate are related but different

FPS describes how quickly the game or application produces frames. Refresh rate, measured in hertz, describes how often the display can refresh. Producing 144 FPS on a 60 Hz display does not turn the panel into 144 Hz, while a 144 Hz display cannot guarantee the game will consistently render 144 FPS. Technologies such as variable refresh rate and frame caps can also affect how delivered frames are presented.

How to use the number when tuning performance

Compare frame time before and after a graphics or resolution change. Moving from 60 FPS to 90 FPS reduces the average frame budget from about 16.67 ms to 11.11 ms. For serious performance analysis, record a real frame-time graph and inspect consistency as well as the average; this calculator is a conversion aid, not a full benchmark.
