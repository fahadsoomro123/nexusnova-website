Review the draft against the product

A useful policy starts with an inventory of what the product collects, why it is needed, which providers receive it, where it is stored, how long it remains and how deletion works. Account records, payments, analytics, support tools and mobile SDKs may create separate data flows. Remove any sample statement that does not describe your real system.

Terms should describe actual service scope, user responsibilities, intellectual property, billing, cancellation, support and termination. If payment or account deletion works through an app store or provider, the document must describe that process correctly. Do not promise a cancellation or rights-request route unless it is implemented and tested.

The generator creates an editable starting point only. Confirm applicable law, recipients, retention, legal basis and rights routes with appropriate review. Use the SaaS and mobile app privacy checklist to compare the draft with product behavior. A generated document is not legal advice.

Worked example: document an actual app data flow

Imagine a mobile app collects an email address at account creation, sends crash diagnostics to a logging vendor, processes subscriptions through a billing provider and stores uploaded files with a cloud host. A privacy notice that says “we never share personal information” would misdescribe that product. The draft should state the real purposes, the role of relevant service providers, the retention rule and the contact route for rights requests. If a provider also uses data for its own independent purposes, describe and review that role rather than calling every recipient a processor.

The retention field should represent an operational rule, not an invented promise. For example, say how long an account record remains after closure, whether billing records must be kept for legal reasons, and how backup copies expire. Verify that the deletion process matches the language. Likewise, if a notice says users can turn off analytics, test that choice in the app and inspect whether optional tags stop or change behavior where required. A privacy document cannot fix a system that does something different.

Review the terms against checkout and support

Before publication, compare terms with the actual price, billing interval, free-trial conversion, cancellation route, refund rules and store-specific payment model. Confirm which entity contracts with the customer and how support can be reached. Replace every bracketed placeholder, remove features that are not present and have jurisdiction-specific clauses reviewed by a qualified adviser. The generated text is editable so a team can review it, but no template can independently identify all laws, data flows, processors or consumer obligations that apply to a real product.

Frequently asked questions

Can I publish the generated documents as-is?

No. Replace placeholders, compare every statement with your real data flows and legal terms, and arrange jurisdiction-appropriate review before publication.

What information should I enter?

Use the actual data collected, providers, retention rules, features, operator identity and a monitored contact address. Do not invent a compliance practice.

Does the generator certify legal compliance?

No. It creates editable templates, not legal advice or a compliance certification. Obligations depend on the product, organization and markets served.
