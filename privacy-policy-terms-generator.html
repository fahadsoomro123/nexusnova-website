Tool methodology

How to turn the draft into an accurate product document

A privacy policy is a description of actual processing, not a generic promise about the product you hope to build. Before generating it, make a short data inventory: what users enter, what the app collects automatically, what stays on the device, what is sent to a server, which vendors receive it, why they receive it, who can access it and how long it is retained. Include account data, diagnostics, analytics, payments, uploads, location or advertising only where those practices genuinely apply. Do not publish unverified vendor names or retention claims.

Verify the inputs against the released product

The checkboxes are drafting prompts, not a code scanner. Compare every selected practice with the shipped code, app SDKs, hosting configuration and vendor contracts. Test whether optional analytics stops when a user rejects it, whether an account-deletion request actually deletes the promised records, and whether backups or accounting logs have different retention. Where a processor or independent provider receives information, document the transfer and purpose accurately. A “we never share data” sentence is not appropriate unless the technical behavior and contracts support it.

Choose the right document and review the clauses

A privacy policy should explain collection, purposes, sharing, retention, transfers, security and applicable user choices. Terms of service cover service boundaries, eligibility, acceptable use, user content, payment or cancellation flows, availability, termination and dispute handling when relevant. The correct provisions depend on the product and market, and a single jurisdiction field cannot determine legal coverage. The FTC’s mobile-app guidance is a useful US starting point, not a global compliance certificate.

Replace every bracketed placeholder, confirm company and contact details, align the document with checkout and support workflows, check app-store privacy declarations and make the policy easy to find before account creation or data submission. Have qualified counsel review terms that allocate significant commercial risk or serve several jurisdictions. Copy and download preserve an editable draft; they do not make its statements legally sufficient. This tool is for drafting and checklist support only, not legal advice, a compliance audit or a guarantee that an app meets any law or store policy.

Frequently asked questions

Can I publish the output without review?

That is not recommended. The tool cannot inspect source code, SDKs, vendor contracts or determine legal compliance.

Does it know my app's actual practices?

Only to the extent your inputs are accurate. Verify every statement against the released product.

Does one selected country determine all legal duties?

No. Obligations can depend on your entity, users, data and market. Arrange suitable jurisdiction-specific review.
