How the calculator works

The calculation is progressive. It first converts a monthly taxable salary to an annual amount when needed, finds the applicable annual slab, adds the fixed tax accumulated from the lower slabs, then applies the marginal rate only to the amount above that slab's lower threshold.

For example, an annual taxable salary of Rs 2.2 million produces Rs 116,000 under the table: Rs 6,000 accumulated through Rs 1.2 million plus 11% of the next Rs 1 million.

Why this tool does not calculate every Pakistan tax case

This salaried-person table is intended for cases where salary income exceeds 75% of taxable income. Mixed-income cases should use the broader FBR rules instead of treating this salary-only estimate as the final liability.

Pakistan income tax depends on the nature and composition of income. A salaried-person slab calculator is not a substitute for a full return computation involving business income, professional income, property, capital gains, foreign-source income, tax credits, minimum taxes, final taxes or other special regimes. Restricting this page to salaried taxable income avoids presenting a falsely precise result for cases that belong under different rules.

How to use the Pakistan Salary Tax Calculator

  1. Confirm the tax year or reference period shown by the page before entering salary figures.
  2. Enter the salary amount in the period expected by the calculator and review the estimated taxable amount, tax and take-home context shown by the result.
  3. Compare the estimate with the current FBR rules, payroll treatment and any allowances or deductions that apply to your real employment situation.

Practical example

If two employees earn the same headline annual salary but one has taxable allowances or payroll adjustments that the calculator does not model, their actual deductions can differ. Use the calculator to understand the slab-based estimate, then reconcile it with the employer’s payroll statement and the applicable tax-year rules.

Limits and checks

This page is a planning calculator, not a tax filing or legal determination. Tax slabs, credits, exemptions, surcharges and payroll rules can change. Keep the tax-year source with any important estimate and use FBR guidance or a qualified tax professional when the result affects a filing or financial commitment.

Tax estimates depend on the correct year and category

A salary-tax calculator works best as a planning worksheet: identify the applicable tax year, enter the relevant salary basis, and compare the estimate with payroll. Progressive tax tables apply marginal rates to defined portions of taxable income rather than simply applying the highest rate to the whole salary.

Actual withholding can differ because taxable allowances, exemptions, credits, payroll treatment and other statutory details may apply. The calculator output should therefore be checked against the current official rules and the employee's real payroll statement.

Preserve the calculation assumptions

Keep the tax year, salary basis and deductions used with the result. Re-run the calculation when the official table changes rather than carrying an old estimate forward automatically.
