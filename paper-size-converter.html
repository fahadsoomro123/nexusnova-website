Paper size conversion FAQ

What is A4 size in pixels at 300 DPI?

A4 at 300 DPI is approximately 2480 × 3508 pixels in portrait orientation, or 3508 × 2480 pixels in landscape.

What is A4 size in pixels at 150 DPI?

A4 at 150 DPI is approximately 1240 × 1754 pixels in portrait orientation.

What is A2 size in pixels at 300 DPI?

A2 at 300 DPI is approximately 4961 × 7016 pixels in portrait orientation.

What size is US Letter at 300 DPI?

US Letter at 300 DPI is 2550 × 3300 pixels in portrait orientation.

Is 300 DPI always required?

No. It is a common high-quality print target, but the correct value depends on the printer, publisher or destination requirement.

Worked example: A4 at 300 DPI

A4 is 210 × 297 mm. Converting millimetres to inches and multiplying by 300 DPI gives about 2480 × 3508 pixels after rounding. The method is pixels = inches × DPI. Printer drivers, bleed, crop marks and application rounding can change the practical export size, so verify the final document settings before print production.

Paper size and pixel size are different measurements

Paper standards such as A4 and A5 describe physical dimensions. Pixel dimensions depend on output resolution, commonly expressed as DPI. The same sheet can therefore have several valid pixel sizes: increasing DPI produces more pixels for the same physical paper.

This distinction matters when preparing artwork, forms or print-ready documents. A screen image can be large in pixels while still being too small for high-quality printing at the intended physical size.

Choose resolution from the output process

Start with the physical paper size, select the intended print resolution, then use the resulting pixel dimensions. For professional printing, confirm the printer's resolution, bleed and color requirements separately.

Choose dimensions for print or screen

Paper size is a physical measurement; pixels describe an image's digital dimensions. DPI links the two by specifying pixel density for print. You need both the page size and target DPI to calculate pixel dimensions.

Choose a higher resolution when the print workflow needs fine detail. Upscaling adds pixels but cannot restore missing detail, and digital forms or previews may need far fewer pixels than print output.

Before exporting the final asset

  1. Confirm the paper standard and orientation requested by the printer or form.
  2. Choose the DPI required by that destination rather than automatically selecting the highest value.
  3. Check the resulting width and height, allowing for any bleed or margin requirement that the target workflow specifies.
  4. Open the exported asset at its actual size and verify that text and important details remain legible.

Paper-size conversion questions

Does 300 DPI mean the image has 300 pixels?

No. DPI is a density used to relate pixels to physical size. The total pixel dimensions depend on both the page dimensions and the selected density.
