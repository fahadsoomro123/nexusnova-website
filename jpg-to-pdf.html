What this converter does

Each selected JPEG becomes one page in a single PDF document. Choose A4, US Letter or US Legal paper, set the orientation to portrait, landscape or auto, and select a margin. Images are scaled proportionally into the printable area; they are never stretched to fill a page.

Paper size, orientation and margins

A4 is common for international forms and office documents, while US Letter is a standard choice for many American workplaces and school forms. US Legal provides a taller page for long receipts or records. Auto orientation chooses a portrait page for a taller image and a landscape page for a wider image; use a fixed orientation when all pages must match. A smaller margin gives the image more page area, while a larger margin leaves more white space for printing or binding.

Useful situations

JPG-to-PDF conversion can help when you need to combine scanned pages, photographed notes, receipts, forms or multiple image-based pages into one document. The paper-size and orientation choices also help when preparing files for US Letter forms, international A4 submissions or pages that need to print more predictably.

Page order and image quality

The PDF follows the order supplied by your browser, so review the selected files before creating the document. Images are fitted without stretching, but the quality of the final PDF still depends on the source JPEGs.

Prepare images before conversion

If a photo is unnecessarily large, resize or compress it first. The Image Resizer and Image Compressor can reduce dimensions or file size before the PDF is assembled. If you need exact A4 dimensions such as 1240 × 1754 pixels at 150 DPI or 2480 × 3508 pixels at 300 DPI, use the Paper Size & DPI Converter.

Why this version supports JPG/JPEG

This lightweight implementation supports JPEG so it can create the PDF locally without loading a large third-party conversion library. If your source is PNG or another format, convert it first with one of the image tools.

Privacy and limits

The images are processed on your device rather than uploaded to a NexusNova conversion server. The current limit is 30 JPEGs, 15 MB per image and 100 MB combined; very large photographs still use device memory. If a file is rejected for size, split it into smaller PDFs.
