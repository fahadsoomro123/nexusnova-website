Build a resume that is easy to scan.

Focus the page on evidence, relevance and readability instead of decorative clutter.

Published 22 August 2026 • NexusNova

Start with the job, not with a generic template

A resume is more useful when it answers a specific employer's question: why should this person be considered for this role? Read the job description first and identify the experience, tools, responsibilities and outcomes that genuinely match your background. Do not add skills you cannot defend in an interview.

Use a clear professional heading

Your name should be the easiest item to find. Under it, use a short professional title that reflects the role you are targeting, such as "Primary School Teacher," "Customer Support Representative" or "Junior Web Developer." Contact details should be current and limited to useful information.

Write a summary with evidence

A good summary is short and specific. Instead of writing "hard-working professional seeking opportunities," describe your relevant experience, strongest capability and the type of work you can contribute. Avoid unsupported claims such as "best," "world-class" or "expert" unless you can prove them.

Turn responsibilities into outcomes where possible

Experience sections become stronger when they show what changed because of your work. A responsibility says what you were assigned; an achievement shows the effect. For example, "Prepared weekly reports" is useful, but "Prepared weekly attendance reports for five classes and reduced missing records through a standard checklist" gives more context.

Use numbers carefully

Numbers can make achievements easier to understand, but only use figures you know are accurate. Do not invent percentages because they sound impressive. Real counts, time saved, class sizes, customers handled, projects completed or error reductions are useful when they are true and relevant.

Keep skills focused

A long skills list can dilute the important items. Prioritize skills that match the target role and that you can demonstrate. Grouping dozens of unrelated keywords may make the resume harder for a human reader to trust.

Education and certifications

List relevant qualifications clearly with institution, program and completion year when appropriate. If a certification expires, do not present it as current after expiry. New graduates can place education higher on the page; experienced candidates usually give more space to recent work.

One page is a useful constraint, not an absolute law

For early-career applicants and many general roles, one focused page is often easier to scan. People with extensive relevant experience may genuinely need more space. The goal is not to squeeze text until it becomes unreadable; the goal is to remove information that does not help the reader evaluate you.

Check before you send

  • Verify phone number and email address.
  • Check dates and employer names for accuracy.
  • Remove spelling mistakes and inconsistent punctuation.
  • Open the PDF and confirm nothing is cut off.
  • Use a professional filename such as Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf.
  • Tailor the resume for the role instead of sending the same version everywhere.
