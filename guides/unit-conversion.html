CONVERSION GUIDE

Unit conversion made practical

Editorial owner: NexusNova Editorial Team

Understand conversion factors for length, weight, temperature and digital storage with formulas and checkable examples.

Published 22 August 2026 • Updated 24 August 2026 • NexusNova Editorial Team

Unit conversion works by expressing the same physical quantity in a different scale. The safest method is to convert through a known base factor rather than memorizing many separate shortcuts.

Length: convert through meters

One kilometer is 1,000 meters, one centimeter is 0.01 meter, one foot is exactly 0.3048 meter, one inch is exactly 0.0254 meter, and one international mile is 1,609.344 meters.

output = input × from-factor ÷ to-factor

Example: 3 kilometers to meters is 3,000 meters. To convert 10 feet to meters, multiply 10 by 0.3048 to get 3.048 meters. Check these examples with the Unit Converter.

Weight or mass: convert through kilograms

For common mass units, kilograms are a useful base. One gram is 0.001 kilogram, one pound is 0.45359237 kilogram, and one ounce is 0.028349523125 kilogram.

If you convert 5 pounds to kilograms, the result is about 2.26796 kilograms.

Temperature needs formulas, not a single scale factor

Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin do not all share the same zero point. Celsius to Fahrenheit is C × 9/5 + 32. Fahrenheit to Celsius is (F − 32) × 5/9. Kelvin is Celsius + 273.15.

Storage sizes can use two conventions

Digital storage is commonly described with a decimal scale where each step is 1,000, while binary-style calculations often use 1,024. The Storage Size Converter makes the scale explicit so you can choose the convention that matches your task.

Avoid rounding too early

When a conversion has several steps, keep more decimal places during the calculation and round only the final answer. Early rounding can create a larger error when the result is converted again.

Pick the correct conversion path

Unit conversion only makes sense when the source and destination describe the same quantity. Length can be converted through metres, mass through kilograms and time through seconds, but a request such as converting kilograms directly to litres requires additional information such as density. A calculator that accepts the wrong physical quantity can still produce a number, so the user has to validate the meaning before validating the arithmetic.

Temperature is the classic exception to simple scaling

For many units, a conversion can be represented by multiplication. Temperature scales also use offsets. Celsius to Fahrenheit is not a simple “multiply by a constant” operation because the zero points differ. This is why temperature conversions deserve a separate mental check even when the interface looks identical to a length or mass conversion.

Decimal and binary storage labels can disagree

File and drive capacities are a frequent source of confusion because decimal units and binary units are both encountered in consumer software. Keep the underlying byte count when comparing storage or transfer sizes and check the convention used by the system or service. A difference in labels does not necessarily mean that data was lost.

Rounding belongs at the end

When a workflow requires several conversions, carry extra precision through the intermediate steps and round only when the final specification requires it. Early rounding can compound across repeated calculations. In formal engineering, laboratory or financial work, use the governing standard for significant figures and required rounding rather than a generic web calculator default.

Turn the number back into the original question

After converting, restate the result in plain language: “2.5 kilometres is 2,500 metres,” not merely “2500.” This simple habit exposes swapped units, impossible magnitudes and decimal-place mistakes. Keep both the original input and the converted value when sharing calculations with someone else.
