Crypto Gains and Tax Records in 2026: A Practical US and UK Checklist

Organize crypto basis, proceeds, fees, disposals and simplified US or UK capital-gains estimates for the relevant tax year.

This guide is a practical working reference. Keep assumptions visible, use the sources below for current details, and verify important decisions against your own circumstances.

Start with records before using a tax calculator

Crypto tax work begins with a reliable transaction history: acquisition date, disposal date, units, proceeds, basis, fees, exchange or wallet, and the purpose of the transaction. A gain estimate based only on the current price may be misleading because taxable events can include selling a coin, exchanging one asset for another or using crypto to buy goods, depending on jurisdiction. A calculator is useful after records have been organized, not instead of collecting them.

Export exchange statements and preserve original files. Record transfers between wallets you control so they are not confused with sales, and retain evidence linking the outgoing and incoming transfer. Mark missing basis as unresolved rather than inventing a number. Some tax calculations require a local-currency value at the relevant time. Keep an audit trail for exchange rates, transaction IDs and later corrections. The effort can prevent an exact-looking estimate from resting on incomplete data.

Understand proceeds, cost basis, fees and realized gain

A basic disposal calculation starts with proceeds and subtracts adjusted cost basis and allowable costs under the applicable rules. If proceeds are $10,000, basis is $6,500 and deductible disposal costs are $50, the simplified result is a $3,450 gain. If the basis and costs exceed proceeds, the disposal may produce a loss. The precise treatment of basis and fees depends on jurisdiction, matching rules and asset classification.

Do not treat an exchange’s displayed profit percentage as a tax calculation. It may ignore prior wallet activity, acquisition lots, transaction costs, currency translation or activity outside that platform. A transfer can move assets between accounts without being a disposal, while an exchange into a different token can have a tax consequence. Reconcile histories across exchanges and wallets, identify every unknown transfer, and resolve unsupported basis before using aggregate figures in a return.

United States: digital assets and holding period

The IRS treats digital assets as property for federal tax purposes. A sale or other taxable transaction can generate gain or loss, and many capital transactions are reported using Form 8949 and Schedule D depending on the facts and statements received. The taxpayer’s records should support the acquisition basis, amount realized and dates used. Read the IRS digital-assets guidance for the tax year instead of assuming that a broker’s report captures every wallet or transaction.

Holding period is important for US federal treatment. Assets held one year or less before disposal generally produce short-term gain or loss; assets held more than one year may qualify for long-term capital-gain rates. Short-term gains generally follow ordinary income rates, whereas qualifying long-term gains can be taxed at 0%, 15% or 20% depending on taxable income and filing status. This is only the federal overview; special rules, deductions, NIIT and state taxes can change the outcome.

US 2026 long-term thresholds are not flat tax rates

For tax year 2026, IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 sets the maximum 0% long-term capital-gain threshold at $49,450 for single or married-filing-separately taxpayers, $66,200 for heads of household and $98,900 for married couples filing jointly. The upper limits for the 15% band are $545,500 for single, $306,850 for married filing separately, $579,600 for head of household and $613,700 for joint filers. Gains above the applicable upper threshold can enter the 20% band.

These figures are not a statement that all gain receives one rate. Capital gains are generally stacked with taxable income to determine which parts fit in which band. This tool uses a simplified federal stacking model and excludes state or local tax and the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax. It cannot fully model deductions, losses from other disposals, special residency rules, trusts or complex reporting. Confirm the current forms, instructions and facts with reliable tax software or a qualified preparer before filing.

United Kingdom: allowance and rates for 2026–27

For the UK tax year starting 6 April 2026, GOV.UK lists a £3,000 annual exempt amount for most individuals and Capital Gains Tax rates of 18% and 24% for many chargeable assets. The rate depends in part on how much basic-rate income-tax band remains after taxable income. The allowance applies to an individual’s relevant gains, not separately to each wallet, token or exchange account.

The 18% and 24% figures are not universal for every possible crypto receipt. Classification may depend on whether activity is a capital investment disposal or income-generating business activity, and UK pooling and matching rules can affect basis. Reliefs, allowable losses, residency and special asset categories can change the calculation. This estimator illustrates one individual disposal and assumes the annual exemption remains available. It is a starting estimate, not a replacement for calculating aggregate gains under current HMRC guidance.

Separate disposal gains from income-generating activity

Mining, staking rewards, employment compensation, business activity and some other receipts can create income-tax questions before any later disposal gain is calculated. The initial receipt and a later sale may be separate taxable events. A single profit number cannot represent both. Keep the transaction type, fair market value at receipt and subsequent adjusted basis where applicable, then confirm how the law classifies your specific activity.

Moving coins between wallets that you control is not automatically equivalent to selling them. A disposal may occur when assets are exchanged, spent on goods or services, or transferred in ways that change beneficial ownership. Decentralized finance, wrapped assets, liquidity pools, forks, airdrops and NFTs can introduce facts that a basic estimator does not model. Record what actually happened instead of relying solely on the exchange label or an automatic classification.

Losses and multiple disposals require a complete-year view

A loss on one disposal does not always erase tax on an unrelated gain without conditions. Jurisdictions have rules about allowable losses, timing, netting, claims and carried-forward amounts. This estimator intentionally models one disposal and does not assume every loss can be deducted freely. Build a transaction-level ledger first, then reconcile it to annual totals and the relevant return forms or self-assessment sections.

When a basis is missing, preserve the steps taken to find it: historical exchange exports, blockchain transaction IDs, wallet files, bank records and purchase confirmations. Do not fabricate basis simply because a spreadsheet needs a value. Mark provisional values clearly and resolve uncertainties with a qualified adviser. Reconstructing a history after an exchange closes can be expensive; keeping supporting evidence as transactions happen is basic risk management, not just year-end paperwork.

A practical recordkeeping checklist

Keep original exchange downloads, a transaction ledger, mappings between wallets you control, basis evidence, fee details, currency conversions, records of rewards or other income, and explanations for unusual events. Reconcile records across platforms and ensure each outgoing transfer has a known destination. Check disposal dates and local-currency amounts, and store the final working paper with the tax year, assumptions, source records and software version.

For US reporting, review the IRS digital-assets portal and current forms and instructions. For UK reporting, review HMRC’s Capital Gains Tax rate page and current cryptoasset record guidance. Prefer official pages over an exchange FAQ because an FAQ may not cover your residency or activity. Never share seed phrases or private keys with a calculator or tax preparer. A legitimate recordkeeping process needs transaction evidence, not control of your assets.

Use an estimator without over-trusting its precision

The [Crypto Tax & Profit/Loss Estimator](/crypto-tax-profit-estimator.html) can check the arithmetic of one disposal: proceeds minus basis and fees, followed by an illustrative rate model. Change the taxable-income assumption and holding period to see why two people with identical gains may get different estimates. Keep the output as a working note and label facts that have not been verified by source records.

Before filing, reconcile all disposals for the entire tax year, apply the jurisdiction’s matching and loss rules, account for income from rewards or business activity, and verify current forms and thresholds. The IRS and HMRC links on this page are the primary sources for the official framework, and year-specific rules can change. If the amount is material or the activity is complex, use a qualified professional. The goal is a result that can be explained and reproduced, not a precise number without an audit trail.
