Markup vs. Profit Margin: Do Not Confuse the Percentages

Calculate markup on cost and margin on selling price and use the right measure for pricing.

Markup and gross margin both describe the profit left above a product's cost, but they use different denominators. Markup compares profit with cost; gross margin compares that same profit with the selling price. Confusing the two can make a price look more profitable than it is.

This guide uses one unit and a consistent direct-cost basis. Treat the worked figures as an example, then substitute your actual unit cost and the costs your business includes in its pricing policy.

Formula and inputs

Unit profit: Selling price − unit cost

Markup: unit profit ÷ unit cost × 100%

Gross margin: unit profit ÷ selling price × 100%

To set a price from a target gross margin, use selling price = unit cost ÷ (1 − target margin), with the target written as a decimal. To set a price from a markup target, use selling price = unit cost × (1 + markup). The two targets are not interchangeable.

Worked example

An item costs $60 per unit and sells for $100. Unit profit is $40, so markup is $40 ÷ $60 = 66.7%, while gross margin is $40 ÷ $100 = 40%.

Now compare two common targets using the same $60 cost. A 40% gross margin requires a $60 ÷ (1 − 0.40) = $100 selling price. A 40% markup requires $60 × 1.40 = $84; that price leaves $24 profit and a gross margin of $24 ÷ $84 = 28.6%.

A discount changes the denominator and the profit. If the $100 list price is discounted by 10% to $90, profit falls to $30, gross margin becomes 33.3%, and markup becomes 50%. Recalculate with the actual selling price after each discount instead of assuming the original margin still applies.

How to apply the result

  1. Calculate the cost per sellable unit, using one consistent definition of direct cost (for example, purchase or production cost and any directly attributable costs your policy includes).
  2. Choose whether the target is a markup on cost or a gross margin on selling price before calculating the price.
  3. For a target margin, divide unit cost by one minus the target margin; for a target markup, multiply unit cost by one plus the markup.
  4. Recalculate at the expected transaction price after discounts and include payment, shipping, returns or marketplace fees in the analysis if they are relevant to the decision.
  5. Check that the resulting gross profit can still cover overhead and other expenses not included in unit cost. A gross margin is not the same as net profit.

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong denominator: Markup uses cost; margin uses selling price.
  • Treating 40% markup as 40% gross margin: On a $60 cost, those targets produce $84 and $100 prices respectively.
  • Applying the list-price margin after a discount: recalculate using the final price paid.
  • Leaving costs undefined: shipping, processing, packaging and returns can materially change the result when they belong in the pricing decision.
  • Calling gross margin net profit: overhead, financing, tax and other expenses may still remain.

Frequently asked questions

How do I price for a 40% gross margin?

Divide the unit cost by 1 minus 0.40. For a $60 unit cost, the calculation is $60 ÷ 0.60 = $100. That leaves $40 gross profit, which is 40% of the $100 selling price, before any costs not included in the chosen unit-cost definition.

Is a 40% markup the same as a 40% gross margin?

No. With a $60 unit cost, a 40% markup adds $24 and produces an $84 price. The $24 profit is 28.6% of $84, so gross margin is about 28.6%, not 40%.

Does gross margin include every business expense?

Not necessarily. It depends on which costs are included in cost of goods sold or the unit-cost definition you use. Operating overhead, financing, taxes, and some selling costs may sit outside gross margin; account for them before treating gross margin as net profit.

What happens to margin after a discount?

Recalculate using the discounted selling price. For a $60 unit sold at $90 after a discount, gross profit is $30 and gross margin is $30 ÷ $90 = 33.3%.
