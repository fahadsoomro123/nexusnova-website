CS2 vs Valorant sensitivity and eDPI: a practical guide.

Published August 23, 2026 by the NexusNova Editorial Team.

If you move between Counter-Strike 2 and Valorant, copying the same sensitivity number will not give you the same mouse movement. The games use different sensitivity scales, so a conversion is needed if you want roughly the same physical mouse distance for a full turn.

CS2 to Valorant sensitivity formula

A common yaw-based conversion is:

Valorant sensitivity ≈ CS2 sensitivity ÷ 3.1818
CS2 sensitivity ≈ Valorant sensitivity × 3.1818

For example, a CS2 sensitivity of 1.20 converts to about 0.377 in Valorant. The reverse conversion brings it back to roughly 1.20.

What eDPI means

eDPI combines mouse DPI and in-game sensitivity into one number:

eDPI = mouse DPI × in-game sensitivity

At 800 mouse DPI and 1.25 in-game sensitivity, the result is 1000 eDPI. This is useful when comparing two setups inside the same game.

Why eDPI is not automatically comparable between games

Two games can interpret the same sensitivity number differently. That means identical eDPI values across CS2 and Valorant do not necessarily produce identical turn distance. For cross-game comparison, use a game-specific conversion instead of comparing raw eDPI.

360-distance is a starting point, not the whole feel

Even when the physical mouse distance for a full turn is matched, the games can still feel different because of field of view, movement, weapon handling, scoped multipliers and visual design. A converted sensitivity should be treated as a controlled starting point rather than a promise that both games will feel identical.

A simple way to test a new sensitivity

  1. Keep your mouse DPI unchanged.
  2. Convert the in-game sensitivity.
  3. Play a few consistent practice drills rather than changing the number after every miss.
  4. If needed, adjust in small steps and write down the setting that feels best.
