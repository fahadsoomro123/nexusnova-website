Battery Runtime: Estimate Hours from Capacity and Load

Estimate runtime from usable energy and average load while accounting for efficiency, temperature, age and reserve.

Estimate runtime from usable energy and average load while accounting for efficiency, temperature, age and reserve. Usable capacity, efficiency and discharge limits vary; follow manufacturer safety instructions.

Runtime is approximately usable stored energy divided by average load, after allowing for losses in the inverter or other power-conversion equipment. Nominal battery capacity is not always the energy that can safely be delivered to AC devices.

Formula and inputs

Formula: Runtime ≈ usable Wh × conversion efficiency ÷ load watts

Keep watt-hours and watts on consistent units. If capacity is given in amp-hours, voltage is also needed to estimate watt-hours; then account for usable capacity, discharge limits and conversion efficiency. Continuous power and startup surge are separate checks from energy runtime.

Worked example

A 500 Wh battery through a 90%-efficient inverter powering 100 W gives about 4.5 hours before degradation and reserve.

Important limitation: Usable capacity, efficiency and discharge limits vary; follow manufacturer safety instructions.

How to apply the result

  1. Find the battery's stated energy in watt-hours (Wh). If capacity is listed only in amp-hours (Ah), multiply Ah by nominal voltage to estimate watt-hours, using the manufacturer's stated voltage convention.
  2. Decide how much capacity is actually usable under the battery's allowed depth of discharge and reserve setting. Do not treat nominal capacity as entirely available if the system protects a reserve.
  3. Estimate the average continuous load in watts. For several devices, add their typical operating watts; do not use only the largest device or assume a motor's startup surge is its normal running load.
  4. Multiply usable energy by inverter or conversion efficiency as a decimal, then divide by average load watts to estimate hours.
  5. Recalculate with a higher load and a lower usable-capacity assumption. Cold temperatures, battery age, discharge rate and device cycling can reduce real runtime.
  6. Check the manufacturer's continuous and surge ratings, discharge limits, ventilation and installation instructions. This arithmetic does not authorise overloading a battery, inverter or wiring.

Common mistakes

  • Treating nominal capacity as delivered energy: Allow for usable fraction and losses.
  • Ignoring peak demand: Check continuous and surge limits.
  • Treating nominal capacity as usable output: check the manufacturer's usable-energy or discharge-limit guidance and allow for conversion losses.
  • Checking only running watts: motors and compressors can have startup surges that exceed the inverter's continuous rating.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a battery last less than calculated?

The estimate assumes a steady load and a representative usable-energy figure. Real runtime can be lower because of inverter losses, a higher-than-expected average load, startup surges, cold temperature, battery age, discharge-rate effects or a reserve that prevents full discharge. Compare against measured wattage and the manufacturer's usable-capacity guidance.

How do I estimate runtime for multiple devices?

Add the typical running watts for devices that operate at the same time, then divide usable battery energy after conversion losses by that combined load. Check the inverter's continuous rating and whether startup surges from motors or compressors could exceed its surge rating.

Should I use the battery's full labelled capacity?

Not automatically. The label may show nominal capacity, while the system or manufacturer may specify a lower usable amount to protect the battery or reserve backup. Use the usable-energy figure for the system and account for any reserve consistently.

Does this formula guarantee the backup time?

No. It is a planning estimate for a representative load. Temperature, discharge rate, battery condition, device cycling and system controls can change the actual duration, so leave a margin for critical equipment and follow the manufacturer's instructions.

This guide is an energy estimate, not battery or electrical installation advice. Use equipment ratings and qualified support for wiring or system changes.
