Credit Utilization: Calculate the Ratio and Manage Timing

Calculate revolving utilisation per card and across limits, and understand why reported balances can differ from current balances.

Calculate revolving utilisation per card and across limits, and understand why reported balances can differ from current balances. The result depends on lender terms, rate conventions, fees and payment schedules.

Credit utilisation is calculated from reported revolving balances and the applicable credit limits. A balance visible in an app today may differ from the balance sent to a credit bureau, and the utilisation on an individual card can differ from the combined ratio.

Formula and inputs

Formula: Utilisation = reported balances ÷ applicable credit limits × 100%

For one card, divide the reported balance by that card's limit and multiply by 100. For an aggregate figure, add the included revolving balances and divide by the sum of their limits; do not mix instalment-loan balances into a revolving-card ratio.

Worked example

A $300 balance on a $2,000 limit is 15%. A second $1,000 limit with no balance brings combined utilisation to 10%.

Important limitation: The result depends on lender terms, rate conventions, fees and payment schedules.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing only by instalment: Compare total payments and fees.
  • Ignoring contract terms: Check rate resets and prepayment rules.
  • Using the combined limits for a single-card calculation: calculate per-card and aggregate utilisation separately.
  • Treating a ratio as a score guarantee: scoring models use multiple factors and reporting timing varies.

Frequently asked questions

Does low utilisation guarantee a particular credit score?

No. Utilisation is only one factor considered by credit-scoring models. Payment history, account age, enquiries and the model used also matter, and lenders may report balances on different dates; no particular ratio guarantees a specific score.

Does one utilization percentage guarantee a credit-score result?

No. Utilization is only one possible factor, and scoring models, reported balances, account history and the timing of issuer reports differ. The calculation can help you compare balances with limits, but it cannot predict or guarantee a particular score change.
