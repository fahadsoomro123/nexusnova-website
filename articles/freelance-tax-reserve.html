Freelance Tax Reserve: Plan Cash Before the Due Date

Estimate a reserve from projected profit and verify local rules rather than applying a universal percentage.

A tax reserve is a cash-planning amount, not the tax bill itself. Start with the expected profit and the payment dates that apply to the business owner's tax jurisdiction, then update the estimate whenever income, expenses or withholding change.

Record the tax year and jurisdiction, expected business profit, other income, eligible deductions or credits, withholding already paid and instalment deadlines. These details can move the required payment substantially, so a familiar percentage should never replace the applicable tax authority's worksheet.

Formula and inputs

Formula: Illustrative reserve = taxable profit × chosen planning rate

Do not treat client receipts, business profit and taxable income as interchangeable. Remove business costs only as the relevant rules allow, and check whether self-employment or social-contribution amounts are separate from income tax. A planning rate is an assumption for cash flow, not an official tax rate.

Worked example

$36,000 projected profit at an illustrative 25% planning rate implies $9,000; that rate is not universal tax law.

Important limitation: Tax rules, thresholds and filing duties depend on the jurisdiction and tax year.

How to apply the result

Separate business cash, confirm deductions and deadlines using official guidance, reserve on collection and reconcile estimates quarterly.

  1. Confirm the jurisdiction and tax year, then open the current estimated-tax worksheet from the relevant tax authority.
  2. Estimate business profit using costs and deductions allowed by those rules, keeping gross receipts distinct from taxable income.
  3. Subtract withholding and qualifying payments already made before calculating the remaining amount to reserve.
  4. Recalculate after a large invoice, expense or withholding change and check the next payment deadline.

Common mistakes

  • Applying another country's rules: Use official local guidance.
  • Leaving out income: Include relevant earnings and credits.
  • Copying an example unchanged: illustrative figures explain the method; they do not replace your own data.
  • Comparing different terms: dates, units and included charges must match.

Frequently asked questions

Can one reserve percentage work everywhere?

No. Tax residence, income type, deductions, credits, other income, self-employment contributions, thresholds and instalment schedules vary by jurisdiction and tax year. Check the current guidance from the tax authority that applies to you and revisit the reserve as year-to-date figures change.

Why can the reserve differ from the final tax bill?

A planning reserve applies an assumed rate to projected profit; a final liability depends on the rules for the tax jurisdiction and year, other income, eligible deductions and credits, withholding and payment history. Reconcile the estimate with current official instructions before each due date.
