Fuel Cost per Trip: Plan a Journey from Distance and Economy

Estimate trip fuel from distance, realistic economy and local price, with a buffer for traffic and detours.

Estimate trip fuel from distance, realistic economy and local price, with a buffer for traffic and detours. Price and real economy vary by route, load and measurement convention.

A realistic estimate uses the full driven distance, including the return journey and planned detours, plus fuel economy that resembles the route and driving conditions. Manufacturer figures are a useful reference, but they may not match stop-start traffic, load, weather or speed.

Formula and inputs

Formula: Fuel used = distance ÷ economy; cost = fuel used × price

For litres per 100 km, litres used = distance in km × L/100 km ÷ 100. For miles per US gallon, gallons used = miles ÷ MPG. Multiply fuel used by the price per matching unit; keep tolls, parking and other travel costs separate if you need the full journey budget.

Worked example

A 300-mile trip at 30 US mpg uses 10 gallons; at $3.60 per gallon the base cost is $36 before tolls.

Important limitation: Price and real economy vary by route, load and measurement convention.

How to apply the result

Use compatible distance, economy and price units; record actual consumption to improve future journey estimates.

  1. Confirm whether the route is one-way or return, and include planned detours in the total distance.
  2. Use one unit system for distance and fuel economy before calculating the volume consumed.
  3. Base consumption on a recent comparable trip or a realistic estimate for traffic, terrain, cargo and speed.
  4. Multiply estimated fuel volume by the current price per unit, then budget tolls and parking separately.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing distance and economy units: Convert to one system.
  • Treating test mileage as exact: Allow for route conditions.
  • Using a one-way distance for a round trip: include every planned leg and detour.
  • Mixing units: never multiply gallons by a per-litre price or kilometres by miles-per-gallon without conversion.

Frequently asked questions

Should I rely on the manufacturer's fuel rating?

Treat it as a comparison point, not a promise. Traffic, speed, terrain, weather, tyre condition, passengers, cargo and idling can change consumption. A recent measured trip using the same units is often a better baseline for planning.

Does this calculation guarantee the outcome?

No. The calculation assumes the entered distance, consumption and fuel price. Route changes, congestion, idling and price movements can change the result, so leave a reasonable contingency for a real trip.
