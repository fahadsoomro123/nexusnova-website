Generator Fuel Cost: Budget for Runtime and Load

Estimate fuel expense from load-specific burn rate, hours and local price, while treating safety separately.

Fuel use depends on the generator's electrical load, fuel type and operating condition. Use a measured burn rate or the manufacturer's consumption figure for the load you expect, rather than assuming the rated maximum applies through every hour.

Record the fuel type, price per litre or gallon, expected running hours, number of operating days and the source of the burn-rate figure. Label whether the rate was measured, supplied by the manufacturer or estimated; that distinction tells you how much confidence to place in the result.

Formula and inputs

Formula: Fuel cost = burn per hour × runtime hours × price per unit

Pair litres per hour with a price per litre, or gallons per hour with a price per gallon. If the machine runs at different loads, estimate each operating period separately and add the fuel totals. Include oil, maintenance, delivery and other running costs separately if you need a full operating budget.

Worked example

At 1.2 litres/hour, four hours uses 4.8 litres; at $1.40/litre, fuel costs $6.72 before service expenses.

Important limitation: Price and real economy vary by route, load and measurement convention.

How to apply the result

Match burn rate to expected load and follow the manual for outdoor location, grounding, refuelling, transfer equipment and storage.

  1. Choose the fuel burn rate for the generator model and expected electrical load, not just its maximum rating.
  2. Match the burn-rate unit to the price unit, such as litres per hour with price per litre.
  3. Estimate each load and runtime period separately; add oil, servicing or delivery only when calculating total operating cost.
  4. Follow the manufacturer's outdoor-operation and refuelling guidance; a fuel estimate does not establish electrical or carbon-monoxide safety.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing distance and economy units: Convert to one system.
  • Treating test mileage as exact: Allow for route conditions.
  • Copying an example unchanged: illustrative figures explain the method; they do not replace your own data.
  • Comparing different terms: dates, units and included charges must match.

Frequently asked questions

Does a generator use the same fuel at every load?

Usually not. Many generators burn less fuel at a lighter load than at their rated output, but the relationship depends on the engine and model. Use the manufacturer's load-specific fuel curve or a measured rate where possible, and treat an unverified single figure as an estimate.

What changes a generator's actual fuel bill?

The result estimates fuel only from the entered burn rate, runtime and price. Actual use can change with electrical load, engine condition, fuel type, start-up periods and changing fuel prices; oil, servicing and delivery are not included unless added separately.
