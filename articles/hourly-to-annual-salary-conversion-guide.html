How to Convert Hourly Pay to an Annual Salary

Convert an hourly wage into weekly, monthly, and annual gross pay, while accounting for unpaid time and benefits.

Start with the hours you are actually paid

An offer of $32 per hour can look equivalent to a $66,560 salary, but only if the worker is paid for 40 hours every week for 52 weeks. That is 2,080 paid hours in a full year: 40 × 52. Unpaid holidays, seasonal closures, unpaid leave, variable shifts, or time between contracts reduce the annual total. The conversion is a planning shortcut, not a promise that the same amount will land in a bank account.

The basic hourly-to-salary formula

Annual gross pay = hourly rate × paid hours per week × paid weeks per year.

For a conventional full-time schedule, multiply the rate by 2,080. At $32 an hour, $32 × 2,080 = $66,560 gross annual pay. Weekly gross at 40 hours is $1,280. A simple monthly average is $66,560 ÷ 12 = about $5,546.67 before taxes and deductions. The actual paycheck will differ depending on the employer's pay schedule and the number of working days in a particular period.

Adjust for unpaid time and variable hours

If $32 per hour is paid for 47 weeks rather than 52, annual gross is $32 × 40 × 47 = $60,160. If weekly paid hours average 32, then $32 × 32 × 52 = $53,248. The relevant inputs are paid hours and paid weeks, not a generic label such as full-time or part-time. For shift work, use a realistic average from recent schedules rather than a particularly strong week.

Compare compensation, not just the headline number

A salaried role may include paid leave, health insurance, retirement matching, a predictable paycheck, or a bonus. An hourly role may offer overtime, shift differentials, or a more flexible schedule. These items do not all have equal cash value, and eligibility varies by employer and law. Compare the expected annual pay, benefit costs, schedule reliability, commuting time, and the value of any paid leave before treating two offers as equal.

Gross salary also is not take-home pay. Federal and state income-tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, and other deductions can reduce the deposited amount. Filing status and personal circumstances affect withholding. For US tax rules, verify the appropriate year and filing status with the IRS rather than applying a single guessed tax percentage to gross pay.

Employees and freelancers should not use identical assumptions

An employee generally is paid for the shifts or hours defined by the job, subject to the employment agreement and applicable rules. A freelancer has nonbillable time: proposals, meetings, bookkeeping, marketing, revisions, and unpaid gaps between clients. A contractor charging $50 per billable hour for 25 billable hours a week does not earn the same gross revenue as someone paid 40 hours a week at $50.

For freelance pricing, estimate the number of genuinely billable hours, annual business costs, tax reserves, and desired income before setting a rate. Revenue is not personal take-home pay. Keep a separate record of business expenses and funds reserved for tax obligations.

Questions to settle before accepting an offer

Is annualized hourly pay the same as salary?

No. It is the annualized amount under the hours assumption. Actual earnings depend on paid time, overtime, time off, and scheduling.

Should I divide annual pay by 12?

That gives a useful monthly average. Actual semi-monthly, biweekly, and weekly paychecks follow the employer's pay schedule.

Should taxes be estimated from gross pay?

Start with gross pay, but estimate deductions and tax using your real circumstances. A gross-pay conversion alone cannot calculate take-home pay.
