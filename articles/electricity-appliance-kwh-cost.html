Electricity Cost per Appliance: Convert Watts into kWh

Estimate appliance energy from average power draw, runtime and tariff rather than treating rated watts as continuous use.

Estimate appliance energy from average power draw, runtime and tariff rather than treating rated watts as continuous use. Tariff structures and equipment use differ; verify the current bill and follow safety guidance.

An appliance label may show rated or maximum power, not the average draw over a whole day. Thermostats, compressor cycles, settings and standby modes can make real consumption differ; a suitably rated plug-in meter or device-level energy reading can improve the estimate.

Formula and inputs

Formula: Energy kWh = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours

Use average watts × hours per day ÷ 1,000 for daily kWh, then multiply by the days used and the applicable marginal energy rate. Keep fixed monthly charges, tiered rates, taxes and other bill items separate unless the goal is to estimate the full bill.

Worked example

A 150 W fan used eight hours consumes 1.2 kWh; at $0.20/kWh that's $0.24 daily before fixed charges.

How to apply the result

Convert measured watts to kWh, use realistic hours and the applicable marginal rate, then compare with meter data.

  1. Check the appliance label for watts and estimate realistic hours used per day, including standby operation where it matters.
  2. Convert watts to kilowatts, multiply by hours to estimate kWh, then multiply by the applicable per-kWh rate.
  3. Use the tariff that applies to the household and keep fixed charges, taxes, tiered blocks or time-of-use periods separate.
  4. Compare the estimate with a plug meter or bill where available, and label usage assumptions that were not directly measured.

Common mistakes

  • Using rated power as constant draw: Measure typical use where possible.
  • Ignoring fixed or tiered charges: Separate marginal cost.
  • Treating rated wattage as constant draw: use average measured power where the appliance cycles or changes modes.
  • Treating the energy rate as the whole bill: separate energy charges from fixed fees, tiers and taxes.

Frequently asked questions

Why does measured consumption differ from an appliance label?

The label may report rated or maximum input while the device cycles, changes operating mode or draws standby power. Measure representative use when possible and compare it with the correct tariff period; do not open electrical equipment to take a measurement.

Does this calculation guarantee the outcome?

No. The result uses the entered average wattage, runtime and tariff. Duty cycles, standby draw, tiered prices, taxes and fixed charges can make the bill differ from a simple energy-only estimate.
