Time-of-Use Electricity: Estimate Savings from Shifting Loads

Calculate savings from moving flexible appliances to a cheaper tariff period and verify the actual rate window.

Time-of-use tariffs charge different prices for electricity used in different periods. Shifting a flexible load, such as a dishwasher or laundry cycle, from a higher-priced window to a lower-priced one can reduce the variable energy charge, but the saving depends on the actual tariff schedule and the amount of energy shifted.

Use the rate table for your exact utility plan and check which days and hours each rate applies. A lower off-peak price does not automatically reduce fixed charges, and tariffs with demand charges, tiered blocks or minimum fees need a broader comparison than a single per-kWh calculation.

Formula and inputs

Gross energy-charge saving: shifted kWh × (old period rate − new period rate).

Net estimated saving: gross energy-charge saving − extra charges caused by shifting, if any. Use the incremental rates that would actually apply to the same kWh under each schedule, and keep both rates in the same currency and per-kWh units.

The calculation assumes the appliance completes the same task and consumes roughly the same energy after shifting. If a different setting, longer run time or additional heating/cooling changes consumption, include that difference rather than attributing all of the bill change to timing.

Worked example

Suppose 6 kWh of flexible use can move from a $0.32/kWh period to a $0.18/kWh period. The rate difference is $0.14 per kWh, so the gross energy-charge saving is 6 × $0.14 = $0.84 per day. If the same shift occurs on 20 days, the estimated gross saving is $0.84 × 20 = $16.80 for that period, before any other tariff effects.

If shifting causes no additional energy use and there is no affected demand or other charge, the saving is approximately the rate difference multiplied by shifted kWh. If the utility charges a demand fee, changes the rate block, or requires the load to run longer, the net result can be smaller. Use the bill and tariff terms to decide which conditions apply.

How to apply the result

  1. Read the current utility rate schedule, including off-peak, shoulder and peak periods, weekdays, weekends and seasonal dates.
  2. Estimate how many kWh the appliance uses for one normal cycle or run using a meter, reliable device data or a documented estimate.
  3. Check which rate would apply before and after shifting that same energy use.
  4. Multiply the shifted kWh by the rate difference, then account for any extra consumption, demand charges or other tariff effects that the shift could cause.
  5. Test one appliance first and compare bills or meter data over comparable periods. Note changes in weather, occupancy and total usage before attributing the full difference to time shifting.
  6. Keep safety, manufacturer requirements, noise limits and household needs in view; only shift tasks that are safe and practical to run at the proposed time.

Common mistakes

  • Using a generic off-peak time: periods differ by provider, plan, day and season.
  • Using the average bill rate: the incremental rate for the shifted units is more relevant.
  • Assuming the whole bill will fall: fixed charges may remain unchanged.
  • Ignoring demand or tiered charges: moving multiple loads together can affect the tariff calculation.
  • Changing appliance settings and timing together: keep the task and energy use comparable when measuring the benefit.

Frequently asked questions

Can shifting usage lower every part of the bill?

No. It can reduce the variable energy charge for the shifted kWh, but standing charges and other fixed fees usually do not change just because an appliance runs at another time. Demand, tiered or minimum charges can also affect the overall result under your plan.

How do I know which hours are off-peak?

Use the current tariff schedule from your electricity provider. The applicable hours can depend on the plan, season, day of week and holidays; do not assume a generic nighttime window applies to your meter.

Will shifting always save money?

No. The saving depends on the rate difference and the kWh moved. If the task consumes more energy after shifting, triggers another charge or does not actually occur during the cheaper window, the net saving may be smaller or zero.

Should I run several appliances at the same time?

Not automatically. Under a tariff with demand charges or a peak-load limit, running many devices together can increase the billed peak. Check your tariff and shift loads in a way that fits the equipment's safe operating instructions.

This guide uses illustrative rates. Check your current utility tariff and appliance usage before making changes.
