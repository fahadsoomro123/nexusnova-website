Google Preferred Sources in 2026: what website owners should know.

Google now lets readers choose websites they want to see more prominently as preferred sources. In August 2026, Google also documented a custom site button and a simple deeplink that publishers can use to help readers make that choice.

What is Google Preferred Sources?

Preferred Sources is a user-controlled Google Search feature. When someone chooses a website as a preferred source, content from that source can be highlighted for that user in eligible Google experiences. Google says preferred sources can appear in Top Stories and can also be highlighted in AI Mode and AI Overviews where those features are available.

Is it a general ranking boost?

No. This is not a switch that makes a site rank higher for everyone. The benefit applies to users who deliberately select the source. Normal crawling, indexing, helpful content, links, page experience and other Search systems still matter.

What changed in August 2026?

On August 20, 2026, Google updated its documentation with instructions for a custom interactive button that publishers can place on their sites. Google also documents a deeplink option for sites that prefer a normal link instead of JavaScript.

Who can use it?

Google says domain-level and subdomain-level sites are eligible for the source-preferences tool. A subdirectory by itself is not treated as a separate preferred source. Availability is global for Top Stories in supported Google Search languages, with Preferred Sources also available in AI Mode and AI Overviews where those experiences are offered.

Follow NexusNova in Google: If NexusNova Tools appears for your Google account in the source-preferences tool, you can add nexusnovatools.com as a Preferred Source. The choice is controlled by you in Google.

What should publishers do besides adding a button?

Do not treat Preferred Sources as a substitute for useful publishing. Google's 2026 guidance for generative AI features emphasizes valuable, unique and non-commodity content. For a practical site, that means publishing original explanations, transparent tool limitations, tested workflows and pages that genuinely solve a task instead of mass-producing near-duplicate keyword pages.

Why this matters for smaller websites

A smaller publication may not have the backlink profile or brand demand of a large publisher. Preferred Sources gives loyal readers a direct way to signal that they want to see that publication more often in certain Google surfaces. The realistic goal is therefore to earn repeat readers first, then make the preference option easy to find.

FAQ

Does choosing a preferred source guarantee every article will appear?

No. Google describes the feature as making content from a selected source more likely to appear or be highlighted in eligible experiences. It does not guarantee placement for every query or article.

Do I need special SEO markup for Preferred Sources?

Google's documented implementation is a user-facing button or deeplink. Normal Search technical requirements and content best practices still apply.

Should I create dozens of pages just to target AI search?

No. Google's current guidance specifically favors valuable, unique content and warns against manipulative scaled content. A smaller number of genuinely useful pages is a stronger long-term strategy than mass-generated query variants.
