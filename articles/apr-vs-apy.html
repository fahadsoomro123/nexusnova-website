APR vs. APY: Compare Borrowing Costs and Savings Returns

Understand APR and APY, including compounding and fees, before comparing loan and savings offers.

APR and APY answer related but different questions. APR is commonly used to describe the annualized cost of borrowing, while APY expresses the effective annual yield after compounding. A comparison is only meaningful when the rate definition, fees, term and compounding schedule are aligned.

What changes the answer?

For a periodic rate r compounded n times per year, the effective annual yield is (1 + r/n)^n − 1. APR and APY should not be treated as interchangeable labels. Loan agreements may define APR using charges and conventions that differ by product and jurisdiction.

Worked example

A nominal 12% annual rate compounded monthly uses a 1% monthly periodic rate. Its effective annual yield is about 12.68%, not 12%. That 0.68 percentage-point difference comes from compounding, before considering product-specific fees.

When the distinction matters

SituationWhat to compareWhy
Savings accountAPY and balance assumptionsCompounding affects realized yield
Loan offerAPR, fees and total repaymentThe headline rate is not the whole cost
Different termsShared time horizonShort and long offers are otherwise misleading

How to compare an offer

  1. Record the stated rate, compounding frequency, fees, term and payment schedule.
  2. Convert rates to a common annual basis only when the underlying definitions support that comparison.
  3. Calculate a conservative scenario if fees, balances or timing are uncertain.
  4. Compare the result with the original lender or savings-provider disclosure.

Common mistakes

  • Calling APR and APY the same thing: compounding and product definitions can differ.
  • Ignoring fees: a lower headline rate can still produce a higher total cost.
  • Comparing different horizons: use the same period and assumptions.
  • Using the example as a quote: illustrative arithmetic does not replace current provider terms.

Frequently asked questions

Can I compare savings APY directly with loan APR?

Not as a complete borrowing-versus-saving decision. Check fees, taxes, payment timing, liquidity, term and the exact definitions used by each provider.

Does compounding always make the effective rate higher?

For a positive nominal rate with positive compounding periods, compounding increases the effective annual result relative to the simple nominal rate. Actual product costs or returns can still differ because of fees, timing and other terms.
