Emergency Fund Size: Build a Target That Fits Your Life

Calculate a cash reserve from essential monthly expenses, income stability and household risk.

Calculate a cash reserve from essential monthly expenses, income stability and household risk. Use current records and clear assumptions; illustrative numbers are not individual advice.

A useful estimate makes its assumptions visible. Record the date, units, source and period for each input. Replace illustrative values with your own current statement, quote, contract, meter or budget before using the result to make a commitment.

Formula and inputs

Formula: Target = essential monthly expenses × target months

Keep the inputs on the same basis. Mixing monthly and annual periods, unlike units, gross and take-home income, or fixed and per-unit charges can make correct arithmetic misleading. When an input is uncertain, model a range rather than hiding it.

Worked example

Essentials of $2,400 each month and a four-month target imply $9,600. Keep a separate known repair fund so the same cash is not promised twice.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing periods: Keep monthly and annual inputs consistent.
  • Copying an example: Replace illustrative values with current records.
  • Copying an example unchanged: illustrative figures explain the method; they do not replace your own data.
  • Comparing different terms: dates, units and included charges must match.
