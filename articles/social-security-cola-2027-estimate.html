2027 Social Security COLA: Official 3.6% Rate, Dates and Benefit Examples

Editorial owner: NexusNova Editorial Team

The Social Security Administration determined the 2027 cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) at 3.6% on October 14, 2026. Earlier media estimates are now superseded by the official rate. This guide explains the announcement, the difference from a long-range actuarial assumption, and how to estimate a benefit change without mistaking a calculation for a personal payment guarantee.

Published October 12, 2026•Reviewed against public sources
Official update (October 14, 2026): The SSA's COLA summary says it determined a 3.6% COLA on October 14, 2026. The SSA's benefits effect page says the December 2026 adjustment is first payable in January 2027. Earlier estimates of 3.4%–3.6% were forecasts before the official announcement. The separate 2.4% entry in the 2026 Trustees Report is a long-range actuarial assumption, not the official annual COLA. Read the official SSA COLA summary and benefit effect details.

What is the official 2027 Social Security COLA?

The official 2027 adjustment is 3.6%, according to the SSA's COLA summary, which records that it was determined on October 14, 2026. Earlier consumer-finance reports put the likely rate between 3.4% and 3.6%; those reports were forecasts made before the SSA determination, not the final source of record.

The difference between the official annual adjustment and the SSA Trustees projection matters. The Trustees Report uses assumptions to model Social Security’s finances over many years. The annual COLA, by contrast, is determined from observed third-quarter Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W) data. The 2.4% Trustees figure is a long-range assumption, not a competing official 2027 rate.

How much does a 3.6% COLA add?

Use your own current monthly benefit for an illustrative estimate. For a $2,000 monthly benefit, the official 3.6% COLA equals an extra $72 per month, or $864 over twelve months before any other changes or deductions. For comparison, 3.5% would add $70 and 3.4% would add $68 per month; those were earlier estimate scenarios, not the rate determined by SSA.

How the SSA calculates COLA

The annual adjustment is based on the change in the average CPI-W for July, August and September compared with the average for the third quarter of the last year in which a COLA became effective. The percentage change is rounded to the nearest tenth of a percentage point. This is why the September inflation release is a key date: the full third-quarter data set is needed to determine the adjustment.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released September 2026 CPI data on October 14. The SSA's COLA summary now records the official 3.6% determination; the Agency's COLA effect page says it is first payable in January 2027.

When would a 2027 COLA take effect?

Social Security’s annual COLA is effective for December of the current year and is generally reflected in benefits payable in January of the next year. SSA's effect page identifies the 3.6% COLA as the adjustment for December 2026, first payable in January 2027. Supplemental Security Income timing can differ, so recipients should check their own SSA notices.

What to watch after the announcement

The 3.6% rate is confirmed on SSA.gov. Estimate the gross change using that rate, then compare it with any changes to Medicare premiums, tax withholding or other benefit deductions. A COLA does not guarantee that a person's net bank deposit rises by the same dollar amount.
