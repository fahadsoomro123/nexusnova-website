Checking a discount before you buy

A discount calculator helps separate the advertised percentage from the actual amount you pay. Enter the original price and discount rate to estimate the reduction and the price after the discount, then compare that figure with the checkout total.

Taxes, delivery fees, minimum-spend rules, coupons and regional pricing can change the final amount charged. If more than one discount applies, the order matters: two successive percentage discounts are usually not the same as adding the percentages together.

Practical checks

  • Use the pre-discount price shown for the exact item or quantity.
  • Check whether tax is calculated before or after the discount.
  • For stacked offers, calculate each discount in the order the seller applies it.

Quick FAQ

Is 20% off plus 10% off the same as 30% off? Usually no. Successive discounts are applied to a changing price, so a 20% reduction followed by 10% off the remainder produces a different final price.

Discount percentage is not always the final checkout price

A percentage discount reduces the entered base price, but a store can still add tax, delivery, service fees or other charges afterward. Two sequential discounts also do not usually equal one discount made by adding their percentages together.

For example, reducing 100 by 20% and then reducing the remaining 80 by 10% gives 72, not 70. Use the calculator for the stated discount step and compare the final retailer total separately.

Limitation: a discount result may not be the final checkout total

The method applies the entered percentage to the entered base price. For example, 20% off 100 gives a discounted price of 80 before any later tax, delivery, service fee or store-specific adjustment. Use the result for the discount step, then verify the retailer's final total separately.
