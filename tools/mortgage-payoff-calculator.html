What the mortgage payoff estimate tells you

A regular mortgage payment usually contains interest and principal. Interest is calculated on the outstanding balance, so an additional payment that truly reduces principal can lower the balance on which later interest is charged. This calculator compares two simplified paths from your current balance: keep paying the amount you entered, or add a fixed extra principal amount each month. It estimates the payoff date in months, future interest and the interest difference between the paths.

The formula behind the calculation

For a monthly model, the periodic interest rate is annual APR ÷ 12 (or APR entered as a percentage divided by 1,200). Each month, interest is the remaining balance multiplied by that monthly rate. The amount of a payment left after interest reduces principal. The estimate repeats that process until the balance reaches zero. When you enter an extra payment, the scheduled payment and extra principal are added together for the accelerated scenario.

For example, with a $320,000 balance, a 6.5% APR and a $2,200 monthly principal-and-interest payment, adding $250 every month can shorten the schedule and reduce future interest. The exact savings depend on the entered figures and how the lender credits extra money. Results should be treated as a planning comparison rather than a lender payoff quote.

Before you make extra payments

Check whether your mortgage has a prepayment penalty or rules about how extra funds are applied. Some servicers let you specify principal-only payments; others may advance the due date instead. The calculator assumes the extra amount immediately reduces principal, the APR remains constant, payments happen monthly and there are no additional fees. It excludes property tax, homeowners insurance, escrow, mortgage insurance, servicing charges, rate resets and refinance costs.

Do not reduce an emergency reserve or ignore high-interest debt simply to make an extra mortgage payment. Frequently asked questions

Should I enter my full monthly housing payment? No. Enter principal and interest only. Escrow for property tax or insurance does not pay down the loan principal.

What if my payment is too small? If the monthly payment does not cover estimated interest, the balance will not decline and the calculator will explain that the schedule cannot amortize.

Is the result my official payoff amount? No. Contact your servicer for a dated payoff quote that includes accrued interest and any applicable fee.
