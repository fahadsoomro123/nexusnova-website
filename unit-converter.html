Supported conversions

Length includes metres, kilometres, centimetres, millimetres, inches, feet, yards and miles. Weight includes kilograms, grams, pounds and ounces. Temperature supports Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin.

Worked examples to check your result

Use these small calculations as sanity checks after choosing the same category and units in the converter.

  • Length: 1 mile = 1.609344 kilometres. The mile is 1,609.344 metres, so converting it to kilometres divides by 1,000.
  • Weight: 500 grams = 0.5 kilograms. A kilogram contains 1,000 grams, so half a kilogram is 500 grams.
  • Temperature: 0°C = 32°F = 273.15 K. Temperature conversions use both a scale factor and an offset, unlike length and weight conversions.

Temperature formulas

To convert Celsius to Fahrenheit, multiply by 9/5 and add 32. To convert Celsius to Kelvin, add 273.15. For a conversion in the opposite direction, the tool first resolves the input to Celsius and then calculates the target unit.

Choose the correct quantity

This page converts length, weight and temperature only. It does not convert area, volume, speed, pressure or data storage. For example, 1 square metre is not 1 metre, and a cubic metre requires a volume conversion rather than a length factor. Use a dedicated converter whenever the quantity includes squared or cubed units.

The result is calculated locally in your browser. Keep the original value and unit beside the converted value when copying it into a form, design, recipe or technical note; that makes it easier to spot a unit-selection mistake later.

How the conversion categories differ

Length conversions use fixed scale relationships between units such as metres, kilometres, feet and miles. Weight conversions similarly use defined relationships between kilograms, grams, pounds and ounces. Temperature is different because Celsius, Fahrenheit and Kelvin use both a scale relationship and an offset.

For temperature, first convert the source value to Celsius and then convert from Celsius into the requested unit. This matters because adding or multiplying a temperature by the wrong factor can produce a plausible-looking but incorrect result. The calculator keeps the operation local in the browser so the result does not depend on a remote conversion service.

Check the unit before you check the number

A numerically correct result can still be the wrong answer when the original quantity is misclassified. Confirm whether you are converting a length, area, volume, mass or temperature. Square and cubic units do not use the same factors as ordinary length units, so this page should not be used as a substitute for a dedicated area or volume conversion formula.

For technical or engineering work, keep the source unit, target unit and original value with the converted result. That small record makes the calculation easier to audit later and reduces mistakes when the same number is reused in another document.

Choose units that match the quantity

A unit conversion changes how a measurement is expressed, not the thing being measured. Ten centimetres and 100 millimetres describe the same length; kilograms and pounds describe mass; Celsius and Fahrenheit describe temperature using different scales. Before converting, check both the quantity and the unit labels. A number without its unit can be misleading when copied into a form, spreadsheet or technical note.

A practical conversion workflow

  1. Identify the measurement and its current unit.
  2. Select the destination unit that the form, plan or recipient expects.
  3. Enter the numeric value and review the displayed result and unit labels together.
  4. Keep sensible precision: a longer decimal does not mean the original measurement was more accurate.

A recipe, shipping label or engineering drawing may require different units. Celsius and Fahrenheit use different zero points and scale steps, so temperature conversion is not a simple multiplication.

Benefits and limits

Having common conversions in one place helps reduce mental arithmetic and makes values easier to compare when collaborating across countries or industries. It is still important to verify the category and the requested precision before using the result. Specialist fields may also use their own rounding rules.

Unit conversion questions

Why can a result have many decimal places?

The calculation may retain mathematical precision, but the source value may have been rounded. Use precision appropriate to the measuring instrument and the task.

Can I convert unlike quantities?

No meaningful conversion exists between different physical quantities, such as length and mass. Choose units from the same quantity category.
