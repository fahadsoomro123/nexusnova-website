Pomodoro Timer

Alternate focused work sessions with short breaks using customizable durations.

Classic pattern

A common approach is 25 minutes of focused work followed by a 5-minute break.

Adapt it

Longer or shorter sessions may work better depending on the task and your concentration style.

Build a focus routine

The timer is a lightweight structure, not a rule. Adjust session and break lengths to match the work, then review whether the pattern is actually helping your focus.

Using a Pomodoro timer without making focus complicated

Choose one clear task before starting a focus interval. Work on that task until the timer ends, then take a real break away from the work if possible. The common 25-minute pattern is only a starting point; longer or shorter intervals can be better for different kinds of work.

The method works best when the timer reduces decision-making rather than becoming another thing to manage. If you are repeatedly interrupted, shorten the next interval or remove the source of interruption. For deep creative work, you may prefer longer sessions with fewer context switches.

Practical checks

  • Define one task before pressing start.
  • Use breaks to move, rest your eyes or reset attention.
  • Adjust interval length based on the work rather than forcing one preset.

Quick FAQ

Do I have to use exactly 25 minutes? No. The classic interval is popular, but the useful principle is a bounded focus period followed by a deliberate break.

The classic 25/5 rhythm is a starting point

A common Pomodoro cycle uses 25 minutes of focused work followed by a 5-minute break, but the useful part is the boundary around uninterrupted work. If 25 minutes is too short for your task, a 50/10 rhythm or another repeatable interval can be more practical.

Use the timer to protect focus, not to create more admin

Choose one concrete task before starting, silence avoidable notifications and use the break to move away from the work rather than opening another demanding task. Browser timers can also be affected by device sleep, aggressive battery saving or a closed tab, so keep that in mind for time-sensitive sessions.
