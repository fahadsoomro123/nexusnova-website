EMI Calculator

Estimate a fixed monthly loan instalment from principal, annual interest rate and repayment period.

EMI basics

EMI is an equal monthly instalment estimate for a reducing-balance loan using the entered interest rate.

Before borrowing

Real lender totals can include fees, taxes, insurance or different compounding rules, so compare against the official loan schedule.

Compare offers on total borrowing cost

When comparing two loans, keep the principal and repayment period clear, then compare the monthly instalment, total repayment, total interest and any separate fees. A smaller EMI by itself does not mean a loan costs less; a longer term can reduce each payment while leaving the balance outstanding for more months.

Check what the quoted rate actually represents. Fees, insurance, payment timing, rounding or a variable-rate clause can make the lender's schedule differ from a simple fixed-rate estimate. Annual percentage rate (APR) definitions also depend on the relevant jurisdiction and product rules; this calculator does not infer fees or generate a regulated APR.

For general U.S. consumer-credit background, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains loan amortization and the difference between an interest rate and APR. These are U.S. references, so check the disclosures and rules that apply to your own lender and country.

Worked EMI example

For a principal of 500,000, an annual interest rate of 12% and a 36-month term, this calculator converts 12% to a monthly rate of 1% and estimates a monthly payment of about 16,607.15. Over 36 payments, the calculated total is about 597,857.58, of which about 97,857.58 is interest. The currency is whatever currency your principal uses; the calculator does not perform exchange-rate conversion.

What formula the tool uses

For a positive monthly rate, the payment follows the standard reducing-balance amortization formula: principal × monthly rate × (1 + monthly rate)months divided by ((1 + monthly rate)months − 1). When the entered annual rate is zero, the tool simply divides the principal by the number of months.

How term length changes the result

A longer term normally lowers the monthly instalment because repayment is spread across more months, but it can increase total interest when the interest rate is positive. Compare both the monthly payment and total repayment instead of choosing a loan only because its EMI is smaller.

What the estimate does not include

The calculation assumes the entered annual rate remains fixed and that payments occur monthly. It does not add processing fees, insurance, taxes, late charges, early-settlement fees, changing benchmark rates or lender-specific day-count conventions. It also does not model irregular payments or extra principal repayments.

Why a lender quote can differ

A lender may round the payment, calculate interest on exact dates, charge fees separately or use a different compounding convention. Some products advertised with a “flat” rate are not equivalent to the reducing-balance annual rate expected by this calculator. Confirm the rate type before comparing offers.

Use the result as a planning estimate

This tool can help compare scenarios before you speak with a lender, but it is not a loan offer or financial advice. For a borrowing decision, verify the repayment schedule, annual percentage rate or equivalent disclosure, fees and total payable amount in the lender's official documents.

Compare monthly payments with total borrowing cost

A low monthly instalment can still lead to a high total repayment over a long term. Compare the payment, total repayment and interest, then check fees, insurance, variable-rate terms and other costs that may not be included.

Use consistent loan assumptions

  1. Enter the principal amount actually being borrowed, not the total value of a purchase unless that is the loan principal.
  2. Enter the annual interest rate and the loan term in the units requested by the form.
  3. Review the displayed monthly instalment, total repayment and interest estimate.
  4. Compare offers using the same principal and term, and include fees from the lender's actual offer documents.

A longer term can lower each instalment while increasing total repayment. Variable rates and fees outside the calculator's assumptions can change the lender's actual schedule.

EMI questions and limitations

Is the result an official bank quotation?

No. It is a mathematical estimate based on the entered assumptions, not an approval, offer or repayment schedule issued by a lender.

Why can my lender's amount be different?

Fees, rate conventions, payment dates, rounding, insurance and additional charges may not be included in the estimate. Use the lender's written repayment schedule before signing.
