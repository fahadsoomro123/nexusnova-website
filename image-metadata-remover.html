This practical cleaner re-encodes visible pixels. It is not a forensic certificate and cannot promise removal of every proprietary or pixel-level watermark.

Why remove photo metadata?

Location privacy

Some original camera files can contain GPS coordinates or location-related EXIF fields.

Camera details

Metadata can include device model, software, timestamps and capture settings that you may not want to publish.

Local processing

This tool decodes the selected image and exports a new copy with browser Canvas APIs instead of uploading it to a processing server.

Image metadata FAQ

Does this always remove GPS EXIF?

For common JPG, PNG and WebP workflows, re-encoding from visible pixels normally drops the original metadata blocks. Specialized formats and proprietary structures can require dedicated forensic tools.

What happens to transparent pixels if I choose JPG?

JPG does not support transparency, so NexusNova places transparent areas on a white background before exporting the clean JPG copy.

What image metadata can reveal

Digital photos can carry information beyond visible pixels. Depending on the device and workflow, metadata may include capture time, camera details, orientation, editing information and sometimes location coordinates. That can help organize a library but may be inappropriate to share with a public upload.

This tool focuses on removing embedded metadata from the image file in the browser. It does not erase information visibly printed into the picture, such as names, signs or watermarks, and it does not make an image anonymous in every other sense.

Share a cleaned copy

Keep the original file for your records and process a duplicate for public sharing. When the fields matter, inspect the downloaded copy with a trusted metadata viewer as a second verification step.
