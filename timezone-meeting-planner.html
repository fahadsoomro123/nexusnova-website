Global & International Meeting Planner

Choose one date and time, then compare that exact instant across three world time zones. The planner also flags whether each result falls inside common working hours, near the edge of the day, or outside typical office hours.

09:00–16:59 common hours07:00–08:59 or 17:00–19:59 edge hoursOther times

Working-hours labels are only a convenience guide, not a statement about anyone's actual schedule. The entered date/time is interpreted in your device's local timezone. Daylight-saving rules are applied by your browser for selected destination zones.

Plan international meetings without manual UTC math

International meeting planning gets confusing when teams span Pakistan, Dubai, London, Istanbul, New York, Toronto, Singapore, Sydney and other regions. This tool keeps everyone anchored to the same instant and shows the matching local time in each selected zone.

How to use this global meeting planner

Enter the meeting date and time as it appears in your own local timezone, select up to three destination time zones and choose Compare times. Each result shows the local weekday, date, time and a simple working-hours hint. Use Share meeting to create a link that preserves the exact instant and selected destination zones.

Why the same meeting can shift during the year

Some regions use daylight-saving time while others keep the same UTC offset all year. That means a recurring meeting between, for example, Pakistan and London or New York can move by an hour for one side when seasonal clock rules change.

Meeting time planner FAQ

What is an international meeting planner?

It compares one proposed meeting instant across several world time zones so distributed teams can see the corresponding local date and time before sending an invitation.

What does “common working hours” mean here?

It is only a visual hint based on the local clock hour. It does not know a person's employer, shift, religion, country-specific weekend, personal availability or calendar.

Why can London or New York shift by an hour?

Some regions use daylight-saving time. Their UTC offset changes during the year, so the same meeting instant can appear one hour earlier or later locally depending on the date.

Plan international meetings around local time

Scheduling becomes error-prone when people copy a clock time without recording the participant's city or timezone. A useful plan shows each participant's intended local time so that a meeting at 10:00 in one place is not accidentally treated as 10:00 everywhere.

Some regions change their clock seasonally. When daylight-saving transitions matter, confirm the city and meeting date instead of reusing a fixed offset from an old calendar invite.

Make the invitation unambiguous

Include the date, local time and named timezone in the final invitation. Re-check recurring meetings across seasonal timezone changes because the relationship between local clocks can move.
