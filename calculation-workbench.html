Choose the calculation from the question, not from a familiar label

“15% of 2,500”, “30 is what percent of 150?” and “2,500 changed to 2,800” are different problems even though all of them contain percentages. The workbench keeps those operations separate so the denominator and direction stay explicit.

Money calculations are estimates unless the governing rules are known

Discounts and tip splits are arithmetic. Taxes, regulated charges, currency conversion and financial products can have additional rules that this general calculator does not know. For a formal invoice, tax filing, loan contract or other governed calculation, use the applicable source and rounding convention.

Dates have calendar boundaries

Age is a calendar difference, not simply elapsed days divided by 365. Date differences can also cross leap years and months of different lengths. A leap-day birth date may require an institution-specific rule when the reference year is not a leap year.

Unit conversions need compatible quantities

Length converts to length and mass converts to mass. Temperature uses an offset as well as a scale. The unit mode deliberately rejects incompatible pairs instead of silently producing a plausible-looking number.

Verification habit

Keep the original numbers and the wording of the question with the result. When the calculation matters, perform a second route—for example, verify a discount by subtracting the savings from the original price—or check a date result against the source dates before publishing it.
