Understand the pricing formula

The planning rate uses gross revenue target divided by annual billable hours. Annual overhead is added to the income goal, then grossed up by the selected reserve. Billable capacity is hours per week multiplied by billable weeks per year. The result is sensitive to these assumptions, so do not use a theoretical 40 billable hours every week unless your work history supports it.

The project quote multiplies the planning rate by estimated project hours, applies contingency to labor, then adds project-specific costs. Include discovery, revisions, testing, meetings and handover when they are part of the promise. If scope is uncertain, document assumptions or quote a discovery phase instead of pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

This is a business planning model, not a tax calculation or a guarantee of market demand. Compare estimates with actual time records and seek advice on local taxes. The freelance pricing guide explains billable capacity, scope boundaries and cash flow.

Worked example: what the inputs really mean

With a $60,000 annual income target, $6,000 in business overhead, a 25% reserve and 25 billable hours across 46 weeks, the model first adds income and overhead to get $66,000, then divides by 0.75 to produce an approximate $88,000 gross revenue target. Annual billable capacity is 1,150 hours, so the planning floor is roughly $76.52 per billable hour. This is not a statement about what clients will pay; it describes the rate needed under those assumptions.

If you expect only 18 billable hours a week, the same revenue target is spread over 828 annual hours and the rate rises. That result exposes a real constraint: a business can respond by increasing its price, improving repeat demand, reducing overhead or increasing billable capacity where workload allows. It should not simply assume unpaid work disappears. Likewise, a reserve percentage is a cash-planning assumption, not the statutory tax rate for every freelancer. Your legal structure, deductions, location and benefits change the real calculation.

Make the project quote deliverable-ready

For an 18-hour project, the quote starts with 18 times the planning rate, adds the chosen contingency on labor and then includes direct expenses not already counted as overhead. List research, meetings, quality assurance and handover if the proposal includes them. State exactly how many review rounds are included and what counts as new scope. For uncertain legacy work, a paid discovery stage may be more honest than a fixed quote based on guesses. After delivery, compare actual time and costs with the estimate and adjust the next quote using evidence.

Frequently asked questions

Is the hourly rate a market-rate recommendation?

No. It is a planning floor based on your income target, costs, billable capacity and reserve assumptions. Compare it with market demand and outcomes.

What should count as billable time?

Count project work and include coordination, research, testing and revisions when they are part of the scope. Sales and administration reduce billable capacity.

Why add project contingency?

Use contingency for identifiable uncertainty. Document scope assumptions and approve out-of-scope work instead of hiding unlimited revisions in the fee.
