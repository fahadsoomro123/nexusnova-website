Tool methodology

How to read your hourly floor and fixed quote

The hourly floor is a planning value based on the take-home target, overhead, billable hours, estimated tax reserve and fee allowance you enter. It is not a market price recommendation. Before using it, decide whether the take-home figure represents personal income after the expenses and reserves you intended to include, then avoid counting a direct project cost twice as both monthly overhead and a separate cost. Treat the percentage fields as simplified planning assumptions, not a substitute for accounting.

Worked example using the default inputs

With a $4,000 monthly take-home goal, $600 overhead, a 20% tax reserve and 5% payment fees, the model calculates a gross target of roughly $6,133 a month because 25% of billings is reserved under those assumptions. Dividing that target by 80 billable hours gives an hourly floor of about $76.67. A 12-hour project with a 15% contingency has 13.8 planned hours, so the quote is roughly $1,058 before any additional project-specific costs. This arithmetic helps expose the assumptions; it does not mean every client or task should be priced at that number.

If you can bill 120 hours rather than 80, the calculated floor falls, but only if that utilization is sustainable and realistic after sales, admin, leave, training and unpaid communication. If the project’s scope is uncertain, use a more realistic hours estimate or adjust the contingency based on identifiable risks. Do not lower the rate simply because one client’s budget is small; consider a smaller deliverable, fewer revision rounds or a different schedule instead.

Turn the number into a defensible client proposal

Break a fixed quote into discovery, production, feedback, quality checks, handover and communication. State deliverables, file formats, meeting limits, included revisions, timeline dependencies and what triggers a change request. A contingency is most defensible when it corresponds to risks such as incomplete source material, late approvals or untested integrations, not as an unexplained markup. Compare the quote with actual hours when the project ends and record where the estimate missed. That learning is more useful than applying a blanket percentage to every future job.

Tax outcomes depend on jurisdiction, legal structure, deductions, credits and other income; marketplace fees also vary by provider and payment method. Reconcile the reserve with official guidance or a qualified adviser, and check any platform fee directly with the provider. Use this calculator for arithmetic and planning, then use your own market research, skills, scope and business judgment to choose the final rate.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a market-rate oracle?

No. It calculates a planning floor from your assumptions. Compare scope, market, experience and demand separately.

Why use billable hours, not all working hours?

Administration, sales, learning and leave can consume time without generating billable revenue.

Does the tax reserve equal my tax bill?

No. Tax treatment depends on jurisdiction, deductions and business structure. Use qualified advice for filing.
