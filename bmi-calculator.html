BMI Calculator

Estimate body mass index from weight and height using the standard BMI formula.

Formula

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ height in metres squared.

Important limit

BMI is a screening measure and does not diagnose health, body composition or individual medical risk.

Check your units with a worked example

For an adult weighing 70 kg and measuring 1.75 m, BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.86, usually displayed as about 22.9. If height is recorded as 175 cm, convert it to 1.75 m before squaring it. Using centimetres directly in a formula that expects metres changes the result by a factor of 10,000.

Adult BMI and child BMI are interpreted differently

For adults, BMI is one screening measure based on height and weight. The CDC adult BMI calculator provides adult context, but the number alone does not diagnose a condition. For children and teens ages 2 through 19, CDC uses age- and sex-specific BMI-for-age percentiles because growth changes how the result is interpreted. The CDC child and teen BMI calculator is one official reference. Do not apply adult BMI cutoffs to a child or teenager; use an appropriate pediatric reference and qualified clinical guidance.

What this number cannot tell you

BMI does not directly measure body fat or show where fat is distributed. It cannot distinguish muscle from fat or bone, and it does not account for symptoms, medical history, pregnancy or every factor that affects a person's health. Two people with the same BMI can have different body composition and health circumstances. Treat this page as a mathematical screening aid, not a diagnosis or a treatment recommendation.

For background on the measure and its limits, see the CDC explanation of BMI. For personal concerns or decisions about diet, exercise or treatment, discuss the result with a qualified healthcare professional.

How to interpret a BMI result

Body mass index is a screening calculation based on weight divided by height squared. It offers a quick way to compare a person's weight relative to their height, but it does not directly measure body fat, muscle, body composition or overall health. A result should be interpreted in context rather than treated as a diagnosis or a complete picture of physical wellbeing.

Enter consistent measurements

  1. Measure weight and height using the units the calculator requests.
  2. Check the decimal point and unit labels before calculating.
  3. Read the result together with the stated interpretation and age limitations.
  4. If tracking a change over time, use the same measurement method and similar conditions.

For example, entering height in inches where centimetres are expected can produce a severely misleading result. If a value seems unusual, verify the measurements before drawing a conclusion. BMI interpretation for children and teenagers uses age- and sex-specific percentiles, so an adult BMI range should not be applied to a child as though it were a clinical assessment.

Health limitations

The calculator is a general mathematical aid and does not consider pregnancy, muscularity, fluid retention, medical history or individual risk factors. A person's health cannot be assessed from one number. For concerns about weight, nutrition or medical conditions, seek guidance from a qualified health professional who can consider the broader clinical context.

BMI questions

Does BMI measure body fat directly?

No. It is calculated from height and weight and does not distinguish muscle from fat.

Can I use adult BMI ranges for a child?

No. Children and teenagers need age- and sex-specific interpretation from an appropriate health source.

When to seek an individual interpretation

If a BMI result worries you or conflicts with how you feel, do not use a web calculator alone to decide what it means for your health. A clinician can consider your age, medical history, body composition, activity, medication and other measurements that this simple formula does not capture. Keep the result in perspective and use it as a prompt for an informed discussion rather than a diagnosis.
