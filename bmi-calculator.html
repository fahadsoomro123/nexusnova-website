BMI Calculator

Estimate body mass index from weight and height using the standard BMI formula.

Formula

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ height in metres squared.

Important limit

BMI is a screening measure and does not diagnose health, body composition or individual medical risk.

Guide on How to Use the BMI Calculator

Enter body weight in the unit requested by the calculator and enter measured height in the corresponding field. This page uses the standard Body Mass Index relationship between weight and height. Height must ultimately be represented in metres for the conventional formula, so incorrect unit conversion can change the result substantially. Check your values before interpreting the number, especially when converting from pounds or feet and inches. A small height-entry error can have a noticeable effect because height is squared in the formula.

After entering the values, run the calculation and review the BMI number together with the information provided by the page. BMI is a screening measure based only on height and weight. It is not a direct measurement of body fat, muscle mass, cardiovascular fitness, or overall health. Treat the result as a numerical starting point rather than a diagnosis. If you are comparing results over time, try to use measurements taken under reasonably consistent conditions so that differences in scale readings or measurement method do not create unnecessary noise.

The interpretation of BMI also depends on the population and context. Adult screening ranges are not automatically appropriate for children, pregnancy, or every medical situation, and people with unusually high muscle mass can have a BMI that does not reflect their body composition. The calculator cannot assess symptoms, medications, medical history, waist circumference, body-fat distribution, or other health factors. For a health decision, especially one involving treatment or a significant change in diet or exercise, use professional medical guidance rather than relying on a single browser calculation.

Key Features & Practical Applications — BMI Calculator

The core strength of a BMI calculator is speed and repeatability. It applies the standard mathematical relationship between weight and height without requiring manual calculation, making it useful for learning the formula, checking arithmetic, documenting a general screening number, or comparing measurements taken at different dates. Because the underlying equation is simple, the tool is also helpful for students who want to see how a real-world body measurement can be turned into a derived value.

The formula is BMI = weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. For a person weighing 70 kg with a height of 1.75 m, the calculation is approximately 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.9. The example demonstrates why unit handling matters: substituting centimetres directly into the formula would produce a completely different and incorrect magnitude. Always confirm that the inputs match the units expected by the interface.

Another practical application is trend tracking. A person can record weight and height periodically and calculate BMI at each point to observe how the derived value changes. That trend should still be interpreted in context rather than treated as a complete measure of health progress. BMI does not distinguish muscle from fat, and it does not explain why a measurement changed. Its best use is as one standardized screening number alongside more relevant measurements and professional advice where appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions — BMI Calculator

What is the mathematical formula used to calculate BMI?

The standard adult BMI equation is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. Written mathematically, BMI = kg ÷ m². For example, if weight is 70 kg and height is 1.75 m, the calculation is 70 ÷ 3.0625, which is approximately 22.9. The square on height is important and is a common source of manual-entry mistakes. If measurements are provided in other units, they must first be converted to the units required by the formula. The calculator performs the arithmetic, but users remain responsible for checking that the entered measurements are plausible and expressed in the expected units before interpreting the result.

Why should BMI not be treated as a diagnosis or complete health assessment?

BMI is derived from only two measurements: body mass and height. It does not directly measure body fat or identify where fat is distributed, and it does not account for muscle mass, bone structure, age, pregnancy, medical history, symptoms, medications, or fitness. Two people with the same BMI can therefore have very different body compositions and health circumstances. BMI can still be useful as a broad screening measure in populations and as one piece of information in an individual assessment, but the number alone does not establish a disease or determine an appropriate treatment. Important health decisions should be based on a broader assessment by a qualified healthcare professional.

Are the same BMI interpretation ranges appropriate for children and adults?

No. A simple adult BMI interpretation should not be assumed to apply unchanged to children and adolescents. Growth and development change body composition over time, so pediatric BMI is commonly interpreted using age- and sex-specific reference methods rather than applying an adult cutoff as though the person were fully grown. Pregnancy also changes the way body weight should be considered. The calculator provides a mathematical BMI value; it does not determine which clinical reference standard is appropriate for a particular person. When the age, growth stage, pregnancy status, or medical context makes the ordinary adult interpretation unsuitable, use guidance from a qualified healthcare professional or the relevant clinical reference system.

Understanding the result

BMI is commonly used as a broad population-level indicator because it is easy to calculate. It does not directly measure body fat and can be misleading for children, pregnancy, older adults or people with unusually high muscle mass. For personal health decisions, interpret the number with appropriate professional guidance.
